The method

A 4-week cycle that repeats, better each round. Every other month is a review month. Here's what a cycle looks like:

Week 1
Your day, your words.
Tell me about your day — anything that happened. This sets your baselines (consistency, grammar, vocabulary, flow) and gets us aligned on structure, discipline, and standards.
Week 2
TED talk teach-backs.
Watch a TED talk, then teach it back to me: what you learned, your questions, what didn't make sense.
Week 3
Wikipedia deep-dives.
Read up on a topic, report back. Reading stamina meets real vocabulary.
Week 4
Movies — conversational media.
Watch movies (up to one a day, at least one a week) and report back daily on what you watched. Storytelling is fluency.

Then a review month — you listen back to your own recordings, catch your own errors, and self-correct — and the cycle picks back up, each round stronger than the last.

Questions

I freeze when I have to speak English. Is this for me?

That's exactly who it's for. Most of my clients start out nervous about recording a single voice note. The method is built for that — small daily reps, real feedback, no judgment. Confidence comes from watching yourself show up, week after week.

How much time does it take each day?

One voice note per weekday, 5 minutes max — plus whatever you're watching or reading that week (a TED talk, a Wikipedia rabbit hole, a movie). The weekly 1:1 call is one hour. That's it. Consistency beats intensity.

Will you correct my grammar?

Not with red-pen lists. I diagnose the patterns behind your mistakes — the way your first language shapes your English — and give you one technique at a time to fix them. You'll also learn to catch your own errors by listening back to your recordings.

How long until I'm fluent?

The curriculum is built as an 18-month arc, with a review month every other month. You'll feel real differences much sooner — most clients notice it at work within the first month — but deep fluency is a compounding game, and we play it properly.

What do I need to start?

Instagram or WhatsApp, and the willingness to send one 5-minute voice note. No placement test, no textbook, no app to download.
