🚗 VIN Barcode Scan — prototype

Point the camera at the barcode or QR code on the driver's door-jamb sticker. It reads the VIN exactly — no guessing like photo OCR. Everything happens on this phone: no photo is uploaded or saved.

1 · Open the driver's door

The sticker is on the frame the door closes against — the door jamb. You can only see it with the door open, roughly where the door latch is.

2 · Find the VIN sticker

Look for the VIN line. Most cars print a barcode under it; newer cars add a QR code. Either one scans — point the camera at it below.

Hold the barcode or QR code inside the frame, about 6–10 inches away. Give it a second to focus.

Ready.

In Case Core, this is where staff taps “Use this VIN” — the same confirmation step the photo-OCR result uses today.

How it works: open-source ZXing reader (WASM build) decodes the barcode or QR code printed with the VIN on the certification label. A decoded VIN is then verified with the VIN's own built-in check digit (position 9), and the vehicle is identified with the free NHTSA vPIC decoder — the same public API family Case Core can call.
Limits: the door must be open; worn or dirty labels may not scan; photo OCR and manual typing stay as fallbacks.
